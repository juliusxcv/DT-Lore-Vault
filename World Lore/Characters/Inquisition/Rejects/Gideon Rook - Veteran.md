The Making of Gideon Rook

Origin of a Tempestus Aquilon

I. The Cell

The Inquisitorial holding facility on Tertius Deck smelled of recycled air and sacred oils. Gideon Rook sat motionless on the iron bench, hands clasped, back straight. Even now, stripped of rank insignia and hot-shot lasgun, he maintained the posture drilled into him at Parmenio. The Emperor's discipline did not falter in captivity.

Forty-two standard years. Thirty-six in service. Twenty-four confirmed operations. Seven hundred and forty-three confirmed kills. The numbers scrolled through his mind like a litany. Numbers were clean. Numbers were pure.

But in the silence, other things surfaced.

Fragments. Screaming in darkness. The wet sound of claws on metal. His brothers' voices cutting to static. Then nothing. Then blood. Then—

Gideon's jaw tightened. He forced his breathing to regulation: four counts in, hold four, four counts out. The technique had served him through atmospheric drops at terminal velocity. It would serve him now.

It had to.

Because when he closed his eyes, he saw the priest's face.

---

II. Valhallax

The mining colony of Valhallax-Tertius burned under a pewter sky.

The boy who would become Gideon Rook was six years old when the soldiers came. He remembered very little—the Emperor's mercy, perhaps, or simple trauma. His father's gray Administratum robes. His mother's hands, always stained with ink from helping his father with the endless data-slates. The hab-unit that smelled like recaf and old parchment.

The screaming started at third shift change.

By the time the Astra Militarum finished, Valhallax-Tertius was a testament to the Imperium's will. No one ever told the children why. Corruption, perhaps. Heresy. Xenos taint. The boy learned later that such things happened—entire populations cleansed for reasons the survivors were never permitted to know. Three thousand souls. Ninety-seven salvageable children.

The boy never learned his birth name. The Schola Progenium did not preserve such things. He became "Rook" at intake—a fortress piece, the instructors said. Walling in what must be contained. Moving only straight, incapable of deviation. A name that would prove prophetic. The boy didn't understand then.

The boy who would become Gideon Rook never asked why the soldiers killed his parents. Not then. He was too small, too shocked, too broken.

He learned to stop asking much later.

---

III. Parmenio

The Schola Progenium squatted on Parmenio's northern continent like a clenched fist of rockcrete and iron. For six years, the boy called Gideon Rook was silent. Obedient. Watchful.

He learned quickly: pain followed failure, and survival required perfection. He studied the other children, catalogued which responses earned beatings and which earned rations. He became invisible, unremarkable, safe.

At twelve, his body began changing. At thirteen, something else changed.

The questions came like a dam breaking.

"Why did they kill them?" he asked his instructor during Emperor's Devotion. "My parents served the Administratum. They were faithful. What did they do wrong?"

The shock maul answered before words could.

"The Emperor does not require your understanding, boy. He requires your obedience."

But the questions wouldn't stop. They came in lessons, in drills, in the chapel. Why did the Imperium kill so many? How did they know his parents were corrupted? What if there was a mistake?

The instructors broke him methodically. Punishment details until his hands bled. Isolation in the reflection chambers. Public shaming before his fellow students. Starvation rations. Enhanced conditioning. The questions were heresy, they said. Doubt was weakness. Weakness was death.

By sixteen, Gideon Rook had learned the most important lesson of all: the questions themselves were the enemy.

He stopped asking. He started believing.

And he became perfect.

He exceeded every expectation. First in marksmanship. First in close combat. First in tactical doctrine. He volunteered for punishment drills. He recited the Imperial Creed until his throat bled. He reported deviants among his fellow students without hesitation.

When the Tempestus Scions arrived to recruit from Parmenio's finest, they took twelve candidates.

Gideon Rook was first among them.

---

IV. The 43rd Iotan Dragons

The Emperor's wrath had many faces. Gideon Rook became one of them.

The 43rd Iotan Dragons operated in the Eastern Fringe, where the Tyranid threat gnawed at the Imperium's edge like a tumor spreading through flesh. Tempestus Aquilon—grav-chute specialists, void insertion experts, the Emperor's talons striking from the darkness of space.

Gideon was twenty-four when he earned his Aquilon wings. Twenty-six when he first killed a man who begged in perfect Low Gothic. Twenty-seven when he stopped counting faces and started counting only targets.

The work was righteous. The Imperium's enemies were legion. Genestealer cults festered in hive undercities. Chaos corruption spread through merchant fleets. Xenos infiltrators hid among the faithful. Someone had to cut out the rot.

Gideon Rook's hands never trembled.

At night, sometimes, he dreamed. Fragmented images: gray robes, ink-stained hands, a child's voice asking questions. But dreams were the mind's weakness, and he had been trained to suppress weakness.

The Emperor protects. The Emperor provides. The Emperor demands.

He obeyed.

---

V. Mondrax III

The space station above Mondrax III was a cathedral of metal and void, three hundred kilometers of habitation drums and docking spires orbiting a shrine world of thirty billion souls.

When the 43rd Iotan Dragons arrived, the station was a slaughterhouse.

Lictors and Genestealers had torn through the crew like razors through silk. Bodies hung from gantries, frozen in the void where the xenos had breached the hull. Limbs and organs drifted in slow orbits. The vox-channels screamed with the dying until they didn't scream at all.

The Imperium remembered Mondrax II. Another station. Another infestation. The Tyranids won that time. The station fell, crashing into the planet below. Sixteen billion dead in the impact. Another fourteen billion in the resulting firestorms and tectonic collapse.

The 43rd Iotan Dragons had orders: cleanse the station or destroy it. Do not let it fall.

They chose cleansing.

For six weeks, Gideon Rook and his brothers hunted through the darkness. Hot-shot lasguns lit the corridors like lightning. Flamers turned xenos carapaces into funeral pyres. They moved in silence, communicated in hand signals, killed without hesitation.

Gideon was twenty-nine. He had become something beyond the frightened child of Valhallax, beyond even the zealous believer of Parmenio. He was the Emperor's weapon, perfectly honed, without doubt or mercy.

The station was saved. Not a single Tyranid organism remained. Thirty billion souls continued their worship below, ignorant of how close they'd come to extinction.

The Lord General himself bestowed the commendations. The 43rd Iotan Dragons were heroes. Gideon Rook received the Macharian Cross for valor.

He accepted it without expression. Pride was inefficient. He had done his duty. Nothing more.

But in his dreams that cycle, for the first time in years, he slept without nightmares.

He had saved billions. The Emperor was pleased.

---

VI. Penitent's Vigil

The shrine world of Penitent's Vigil was beautiful.

Gideon Rook saw this even through his helmet's tactical display as the Valkyrie descended through pink-tinged clouds. White marble temples crowned rolling hills. Prayer flags snapped in gentle winds. Pilgrims walked ancient roads, their voices lifted in hymns that had echoed across this world for three thousand years.

The briefing had been clear: Genestealer cult presence confirmed among the pilgrimage population. Intelligence estimated cell size at fifteen to twenty primary hosts, with unknown numbers of hybrids blending into the faithful. The risk assessment was unambiguous.

Mondrax II could not happen again.

The 43rd Iotan Dragons received their orders: total cleansing. Root out the corruption before it could spread. The shrine world of Penitent's Vigil held forty thousand souls currently in pilgrimage. Acceptable losses to prevent billions dead.

Gideon Rook was thirty-four years old. He had not questioned an order in eighteen years.

The operation began at dawn.

The pilgrims died singing.

Hot-shot las-fire cut through prayer robes and augmetic rosaries alike. The Dragons moved through the temple districts with mechanical precision, clearing hab-blocks and worship halls, moving from target to target. Command had been explicit: complete purge. No exceptions. The cult could be anywhere, anyone.

Better ten thousand innocents than one cultist escaping to seed another world.

Gideon's squad took the Grand Basilica. Two hundred pilgrims had gathered for morning devotion. The priest at the pulpit was mid-sermon when the doors blew inward.

"The Emperor's mercy is infinite," the priest was saying, "for those who—"

The first las-rounds killed him mid-word.

The pilgrims scattered. Some ran. Some knelt and prayed, believing this was a test of faith. Some fought with bare hands and improvised weapons, desperate and confused.

Gideon killed them all.

He moved through the basilica like a reaper through wheat. Targeting solutions appeared in his helmet display. Threat assessments. Fire arcs. Optimal kill zones. The training took over. His body moved before his mind registered the targets.

An old woman clutching prayer beads. Target. Neutralized.

A child hiding behind an altar. Target. Neutralized.

A man in priest's vestments raising empty hands. Target. Neutralized.

The last priest—different from the one at the pulpit—found Gideon in the nave as the smoke cleared. The man was bleeding from a dozen wounds, clutching a data-slate to his chest.

"Please," the priest gasped. Blood flecked his lips. "Please, you must see—we're faithful. We're faithful. The cult—there were only a dozen of them. In the undercroft. We reported them. We reported them. This is a mistake. This is—"

He thrust the data-slate forward. Gideon's helmet display auto-scanned it: genetic purity certificates. Pilgrimage registrations. Temple records showing twelve individuals flagged for Inquisitorial investigation. Twelve. Out of forty thousand.

The priest's eyes were desperate, pleading, human.

"We serve the Emperor. We serve Him. Why are you—"

Gideon Rook pulled the trigger.

The priest crumpled. The data-slate clattered across marble stained with blood and ash.

For a moment—one terrible, eternal moment—something cracked inside Gideon's chest.

Then he stepped over the body and continued the purge.

---

The operation took four days. By the end, Penitent's Vigil was silent. Forty thousand pilgrims. Eighteen confirmed cultists among them.

The Imperium declared it a successful preemptive strike. The Lord Commander personally commended the 43rd Iotan Dragons for preventing "another Mondrax catastrophe." Awards were distributed. The after-action reports praised the unit's efficiency and decisiveness.

Gideon Rook received his second Macharian Cross.

He stood at attention during the ceremony, back straight, eyes forward, face expressionless.

Inside, something was screaming.

But he had been trained to suppress weakness. The screaming was weakness. He buried it. Locked it away. Forced his mind back to the numbers: forty thousand targets. One hundred percent elimination. Mission success.

That night, he dreamed of the priest's face.

And his father's face.

And they were the same face.

---

VII. The Hulk

The space hulk designated Throne's Lament materialized from the Warp three light-years from the Bakka System. Standard xenos purge protocols were enacted. The 43rd Iotan Dragons drew the assignment.

Gideon Rook was thirty-nine years old. He had not slept without nightmares in four years.

The boarding action went wrong from the beginning.

Intelligence had indicated minimal Genestealer presence. Intelligence was catastrophically incorrect. The hulk was a nest, kilometers of twisted corridors and fused warship hulls crawling with xenos organisms.

Gideon's squad penetrated deep into the hulk's core. The vox-traffic was chaos: squads calling for extraction, screaming, cutting to static. His helmet display showed friendly icons winking out one by one.

Then something hit them.

He remembered: the darkness splitting open. Claws like scythes. Brother-Sergeant Makros torn in half, his blood painting the walls. Las-fire strobing in the confined space. The xenos coming from everywhere.

Then fragments.

Running. Corridors. Blood. Screaming. His own? Someone else's?

Time... missing.

He came to himself hours later, alone, surrounded by bodies. His brothers. The xenos. All dead. His armor was damaged. His weapons empty. His hands were covered in blood.

He didn't remember killing them. Any of them.

The extraction team found him sitting against a bulkhead, staring at nothing, helmet cracked and display dead.

"Gideon Rook, 43rd Iotan Dragons," he reported mechanically. "Sole survivor. Unit... unit eliminated."

---

VIII. Questions

The Inquisitor's eyes were augmetic, glowing faintly in the interrogation chamber's dim light.

"Tell me again, Scion Rook. What happened in the hulk?"

Gideon's voice was steady. He had given the same report seventeen times. "Tactical ambush. Xenos presence exceeded projections. Unit was overwhelmed. I survived."

"How?"

"Unknown, sir."

"Unknown."

"Yes, sir."

"You were missing for six hours."

"Yes, sir."

"Your unit was killed. Every single brother. But you survived."

"Yes, sir."

"A Genestealer cult operative could survive such an encounter. If they were already compromised. If the xenos recognized them as... kin."

"I am not compromised."

"That's what a compromised operative would say."

Gideon said nothing. What could he say? He didn't know. The hours were gone. The memories were fragments. He could still be faithful. He could still be loyal.

Or he could be a monster wearing Gideon Rook's face, and never know it.

The Inquisitor leaned forward. "Tell me about Penitent's Vigil."

The crack widened.

"A successful operation. Genestealer cult eliminated before—"

"You killed forty thousand pilgrims."

"I followed orders. The cult—"

"Twelve cultists. Twelve. We have the temple records. Your unit purged an entire pilgrimage over a dozen confirmed xenos-touched."

"I followed orders."

"Orders you didn't question."

And there it was. The thing Gideon Rook had spent twenty years burying, clawing its way up from the depths.

Why?

The child's voice. His own voice. The voice he'd beaten down, starved out, trained into silence.

Why did they kill them? Why didn't you stop? Why didn't you ask?

"I..." Gideon's voice cracked for the first time in decades. "I followed orders."

"Do you dream, Scion Rook?"

Yes. Every night. The priest's face. His father's face. The pilgrims singing. His brothers dying. The questions he'd killed inside himself, howling in the darkness.

"No, sir."

But his hands were shaking.

The Inquisitor made a notation on a data-slate. "You will remain in custody pending full investigation. The Ordo Xenos will determine your... purity."

They left him in the cell.

And in the silence, Gideon Rook—perfect soldier, faithful servant, the Emperor's weapon—finally heard the question he'd been running from since he was six years old:

What if we're the monsters?

He closed his eyes.

The priest's face stared back, blood on his lips, clutching proof of innocence.

"We serve the Emperor," the ghost whispered. "We serve Him."

Gideon Rook had no answer.

Not anymore.

## Intelligence Report

INQUISITORIAL ASSESSMENT – SUBJECT: GIDEON ROOK  
Recipient: [[Lord Inquisitor Seraphina Valeria]]  
Classification: Tempestus Scion, 43rd Iotan Dragons — Sole Survivor, *Throne's Lament* Incident  

Seventeen interrogation cycles. The account does not change, which is itself anomalous — the guilty embellish, the innocent contradict themselves. Rook does neither. Six hours remain unaccounted for. Gene-purity screening returns clean; the Ordo is aware such screening has failed before.

Of greater interest than the hulk is Penitent's Vigil. The subject executed a forty-thousand-soul purge over twelve confirmed cultists and asked nothing. The machine his instructors built is now cracking along precisely that seam. A weapon that has begun to ask *why* is either about to become useless — or, for the first time, trustworthy.

Recommendation: neither purgation nor release. The subject's compromise cannot be proven or excluded. Deploy him where a compromised asset would be forced to reveal itself, under an authority prepared to act on the answer.

*[Recommendation amended in Valeria's hand: "Approved. Assign to me."]*
